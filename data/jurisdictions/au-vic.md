# Victoria (AU) – used-car buying rules and costs

Figures below were checked against official or near-official sources on the `verified` date. Fees change every 1 July; **re-verify**.

## One-off purchase costs
| Item | Value | Source |
|------|-------|--------|
| Motor vehicle duty | **$8.40 per $200 or part thereof** of market value (passenger cars up to $80,809; higher bands above) → `ceil(price/200) × 8.40` | State Revenue Office – current rates |
| Private transfer fee | **$44.90** | VicRoads – transfer and duty fees |
| PPSR check | ~$3 | ppsr.gov.au |
| Pre-purchase inspection | ~$200–350 (estimate – get a quote) | RACV / mobile mechanics |
| Roadworthy certificate (RWC) | Required to transfer/register in most cases; seller normally supplies for a registered private sale; must be issued within 30 days of the transfer | VicRoads |

Duty is assessed on the higher of price paid or market value, so negotiating below market may not reduce it.

## Consumer protection (important for cheap cars)
- **Dealer statutory warranty** (Consumer Affairs Victoria): applies only to cars **under 10 years old (from build date) AND under 160,000 km**; lasts 3 months or 5,000 km. Older/higher-km cars: dealers need not provide it; any "extended warranty" is an optional add-on – read exclusions (forced servicing, voided by missed service).
- **Private sale:** no statutory warranty and no cooling-off. Protection = inspection + PPSR + RWC + pay only at handover/transfer.
- Australian Consumer Law protections still apply regardless of warranty.

## Process rules
- Transfer registration into your name within **14 days**.
- Seller submits a Notice of Disposal; buyer shares RWC and pays via myVicRoads.
- **Interstate vehicle** (e.g. bought in NSW): needs a Victorian RWC (issued within 30 days) to register in Victoria; NSW plates must be returned before an unregistered-vehicle permit can be issued if NSW registration is cancelled. Verify current steps.
