## First-year cost model (≈$7.5k car, metro Melbourne)
Purchase $7,500 · duty $315 · transfer $45 · inspection ~$200–350 · history check ~$3 · rego ~$930–960 · fuel ~$1,150–1,400 (10,000 km) · insurance ~$800–1,500 · servicing ~$300–500 · contingency ~$500 → **~$12–13k in year 1** (about $7.9k to acquire, ~$4–5.5k to run).

## What the sweep taught us (generalisable)
- Cheap cars (≤ $10k) are mostly 10+ years old, so **dealer statutory warranty (cars < 10 yrs and < 160k km in Victoria) rarely applies** – inspection matters more than a dealer's promise.
- Brands with a reputation premium (e.g. Corolla) cost noticeably more for the same age/km; check alternatives before anchoring.
- The extra ~$3–4k from ideal spend to ceiling buys a newer platform and safety kit, not dramatically better reliability.
- "Great price" tags mean cheap *for that model*, including 4x4s and luxury cars with huge km – always combine with your own filters.
- Interstate purchases add a roadworthy test, paperwork and transport; they rarely beat local deals unless clearly ≥ ~$1.5–2k cheaper.
- Cars with no roadworthy/rego listed carry hidden costs; compare on year-1 all-in cost and rego remaining.
