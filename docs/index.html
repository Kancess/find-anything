How it works

Research first. You in the loop.

The same six steps for a pram, a laptop or a used car. Only the research and the sources change.

1

Research the category

What matters, typical prices, known problems, hidden costs and local consumer rules, each with its source. You correct it before it searches.

2

Find where it’s sold near you

Not a fixed list. Washing machines, bikes and cars live on different sites, so it looks them up for your location and flags any that look stale.

3

You log in where needed you

It tells you which sites need an account up front and what to do in your own Chrome. It never types your password, never creates accounts, and you can skip any source.

4

Search and vet, read-only

A few narrow queries, one page at a time. Red flags, recalls and an inspection checklist for what you can’t see in a listing.

5

Work out the true cost

Price plus running costs and repairs, minus resale, compared with buying new, borrowing, or not buying at all.

6

Shortlist, then you decide you

A ranked list with a recommendation and a runner-up. It drafts any message to a seller; nothing is sent until you approve the exact words.

Your control

The agent does the legwork. You keep the keys.

The agent

  • Researches, searches and reads listings
  • Vets, costs and ranks
  • Drafts the exact message
  • Stops and asks when it’s stuck. It never skips, bypasses or guesses

You

  • Log in, solve CAPTCHAs, opt in to strict sites
  • Approve every message before it is sent
  • Inspect the item, pay, and decide
  • Skip any source you don’t want searched

It runs read-only in your own browser. It never pays, never creates accounts, and never reveals your budget or deadline to a seller.

Good to know

Used responsibly.

Is it allowed to browse these sites?

It drives your own browser to read public listings you could view yourself: sequential, light, read-only, no bulk crawling. Many sites restrict automated access, social networks especially, so those are opt-in only. You are responsible for complying with the terms of any site you use it on.

Does it work for categories you haven’t mapped?

Yes, that’s the point. The core researches the category and finds the sources itself. Cars are the first mapped “domain pack” with site notes and cost rules; any good hunt can become another.

Can I trust the results?

Treat them as a well-researched head start, not a verdict. Nothing is inspected. Always check recalls and history, and inspect before you pay. It says what it couldn’t confirm. This isn’t financial, legal, safety or mechanical advice.

What do I need?

Claude Code, the Claude in Chrome extension, and Chrome. No API keys and no servers.
